## Prepare the Mac

iOS testing requires **macOS and Xcode**. Open Xcode and finish its initial setup. Start a simulator, or connect a trusted physical iPhone or iPad.

Physical devices may require Developer Mode and the relevant device services. Follow the device's and Xcode's prompts, then check prerequisites:

```bash
pnpm doctor
```

## Keep the device ready

Open the app you intend to test. Keep the device unlocked, awake, and reserved for the recording or Run. On iPad, check that keyboard, window, or multitasking state has not moved the target out of the expected layout.

## Verify pixels and controls

Open **Devices** and select the exact device. You can control it here without creating a Test. Choose **Open app → Choose installed app** to find and open an app by its name. Manual name or bundle-identifier entry remains available as a fallback.

Use **Fullscreen live control** to fit the whole device to the window. Press **Escape** to return; the live session stays connected.

Pixels, touch input, and accessibility are separate capabilities. Relay checks input admission and the current iOS window bounds before enabling taps and typing. **View only** means the pixels are available but control has not been established. Keep the app open, choose **Enable control** once, and check again. If a prior input needs review or another session owns the device, Relay keeps control disabled rather than repeating the input or taking over.

Do not infer tap coordinates from screenshot pixel dimensions: Retina captures and logical input coordinates can differ. Relay uses the current app window's logical bounds for supervised preview input.

## Record and replay

From live control, choose **Record test**, select the App that should own the Test, and choose **Use current screen** to begin where you are. An acknowledged app launch can carry into setup without launching it again.

[Record a short journey](/docs/workflows/recording), save it, and [run it again](/docs/workflows/running) on the same device and configuration. Choose whether to restart the app or begin from its current state.

A control-specific capture belongs to that recorded step, app, and device profile. If it no longer matches, record the affected step again from the correct screen.

## Start with one iOS case

Qualify one short flow on the exact iPhone, iPad, or simulator first. Do not assume an Android recording transfers unchanged: accessible control identities, coordinates, and layouts can differ. Keep platform-specific steps when needed, and compare the resulting screenshots in the same [review workflow](/docs/workflows/review).

## Current limitations

Native capture and control support varies by device, OS, app, and build. Full-flow video is not qualified on every physical iOS path; lightweight screenshots and structure are the default. Capture saving can also be slower than browser execution.

Avoid competing automation clients while verifying a flow. If a connection is interrupted, use the [recovery steps](/docs/reference/troubleshooting) before resuming.
