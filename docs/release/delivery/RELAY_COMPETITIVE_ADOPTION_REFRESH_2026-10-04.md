# Relay: competitive adoption refresh — October 4, 2026

Research for `agent-device-9job`, against Relay `250ab504d3a652ab8722095782594c08ff8503e3`. Official pages were read on October 4, 2026; X was read directly in the inline browser around 19:59 UTC. This note is an evidence input to the existing delivery program, not a replacement implementation backlog. No competitor account was created, product benchmark run, message sent, subscription purchased, or public content posted.

## Assessment

Relay has a credible local record → replay → review foundation. The remaining adoption problem is the effort a newcomer must invest before trusting one useful result. A desktop UI becomes an advantage when it removes that effort. The number of surfaces and supported capabilities alone does not establish an advantage.

The competitive premise needs correction: **e2e is an open testing framework; TesterArmy also sells a hosted visual product. Revyl also has a visual product.** Their paid users already have dashboards, agent integrations, evidence, and mobile support. We cannot win those users merely by adding a GUI.

These are hosted service prices. The open e2e framework is not this subscription.
- [Revyl pricing](https://revyl.com/pricing/): Free; Starter $250/month with $250 compute credits and 3 concurrent devices; Team Pro $750/month with $750 credits and 10 concurrent devices; Enterprise custom. The page lists simulator overage of $0.15/minute on iOS and $0.12/minute on Android when enabled, with LLM usage using the same allowance.

Price alone is an incomplete comparison: include actual hardware, model usage, maintenance and operator time when testing a paid user's flow.

## Why attention and payment happen

### Attention: the million-view claim is real, but limited

The [founder's October 1 e2e launch](https://x.com/o_kwasniewski/status/2105675143464763540) showed **1,182,537 views, 4,847 likes, 611 reposts, 8,630 bookmarks and 204 replies** when directly read in the inline browser. It presents a short category statement, initialization command, five capabilities and a static branded image. It is not a million-view video. These counters do not measure unique people, installation, retention, paid customers or revenue.

The existing [distribution audit](./TESTERARMY_DISTRIBUTION_AUDIT_2026-10-04.md) directly recorded Expo and Guillermo Rauch quoting the launch, follow-up explanations, and creator interpretation earlier that day. An [official Expo guide](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/using-e2e/) supplies an adoption path in an existing developer ecosystem. The plausible lesson is a clear job, easy trial, familiar workflow, inspectable results and trusted distribution. The effect of each factor is unknown; paid amplification and X ranking cannot be inferred from these pages.

Fresh profile reads also show why feature parity is insufficient: [Revyl](https://x.com/tryrevyl) was promoting cloud development and shared screen context; its September 29 Whop quote showed 848 views and its September 27 shared-canvas quote 1,224. [TesterArmy](https://x.com/TesterArmy) paired Expo material with an offer to bring a URL/build and leave a live demonstration with a report. Those are concrete customer jobs, with uneven public reach. No new videos were played in this refresh. The prior [video comparison](./RELAY_SOCIAL_DEMO_COMPARISON_2026-10-04.md) records its actual sampled frames and audio limitations.

### Payment: remove recurring work and uncertainty

The [Novu customer interview](https://tester.army/customers/novu) emphasizes self-serve changes, visible run recordings, real signup/OTP and adaptation when UI changes. Its 50% flake reduction and roughly 30% faster merging are the customer's estimates; the article says they were not formally tracked. The useful evidence is the buying problem: an opaque service and fragile authentication created recurring work.

The [Juno interview](https://tester.army/customers/juno) describes cancelled agent tools that required lengthy pseudocode or stalled while consuming credits, followed by managed cross-platform execution. Its 10× throughput is the customer's estimate. The report also describes a move from 501 to 791 merged PRs with another engineer joining during the period, so it does not isolate causality. The product lesson remains useful: bounded execution, clear failures and reduced babysitting matter more than autonomous-looking demos.

## Focused differentiator to test

**Proposed promise:** “Record the flow once. Your coding agent repeats it. You can see and repair exactly what happened.”

Target a small web/product team that wants to keep tests, accounts and evidence locally and wants an understandable manual fallback. Relay can combine direct recording, deterministic replay, retained screenshots and agent handoff in the same workflow. These mechanisms exist; lower total cost, better reliability and superior usability must still be measured against the buyer's current tool.

A practical next demonstration uses one signed-in settings or checkout flow: create the Test, repeat unchanged, introduce a clearly labeled controlled defect, inspect the exact failure, repair it, repeat unchanged and share the evidence. The human should be able to finish when the agent is unavailable. Start with browser testing while the phone is absent. Bring the physical Android flow back after qualification.

Suggested acceptance experiment: at least four of five unfamiliar participants complete a first saved Test and inspect its screenshot without coaching; the same supported flow repeats across fresh sessions, while a seeded defect consistently fails with actionable evidence. Publish actual times and failures, rather than turning these proposed thresholds into an achieved rating.

The commercial pilot should compare one critical flow side by side with the buyer's incumbent: setup time, repeat duration/cost, auth failures, incorrect passes, repair time, operator minutes and evidence usefulness. Keep the old suite running during the trial. Earn a switch when Relay removes a measured pain. A broad cloud device farm, a larger map and more buttons should not delay that proof.

## Product work from this review

The root agent used Relay's inline browser to open a saved Admin Test, inspect its retained Run and request an evidence export. The existing configuration dropdown already showed the account and destination, so the assessment's claim that all context was hidden in a tooltip was partly stale. This pass makes the existing control explicitly say **Run on**, without adding another toolbar control. Edited recordings now say **Run and save** or **Run and add to…** when saving actually executes the reviewed recording. A directly eligible recording still says **Save test**.

The manual MCP installation examples contradicted the plugin's recommended QA profile. They now use the same explicit QA profile, matching doctor check and discover → connect → run → inspect sequence. The executable's compatibility default stays intact.

One UI evidence export returned the raw message `signal timed out`. The same UI request succeeded on retry; direct canonical and proxy reads also succeeded. Clear timeout, interruption and connection recovery plus an explicit manual retry were added. This does **not** establish or repair the underlying transient timeout cause. A generated download link also does not qualify a recipient opening the report. That remaining work is tracked in `agent-device-od9w`.

A separate repeated screenshot-cache warning was reproduced and repaired: direct image URLs now explicitly skip queries when there is no loader. No relationship to the export timeout was established. The fresh Luna browser probe could not access its inline browser; it provides no novice usability result. Root dogfood and component regressions remain engineering evidence.

## Next release and demonstration

The first customer hypothesis is a small team using a coding agent that wants local ownership and an understandable visual fallback. A suitable switching trigger is recurring time spent reproducing a UI problem, recreating account context or explaining a failed run. This is a proposed target, not a validated segment.

| Delivery                 | Acceptance evidence                                                                                                                                                        | Existing tracking   |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| A supported installation | A newcomer installs outside the repository, opens a workspace and reaches a ready browser without contributor commands                                                     | `agent-device-f0zj` |
| First useful result      | Four of five unfamiliar people save a Test, repeat it and identify its screenshot without coaching; record setup time separately                                           | `agent-device-7zd2` |
| A useful handoff         | A recipient opens the relevant result outside the operator runtime, understands its account and exact check, and can identify missing evidence                             | `agent-device-od9w` |
| A reason to switch       | Three pilot teams compare one existing critical flow with their incumbent; measure setup, incorrect passes, repairs, cost and operator minutes before asking for migration | `agent-device-4gxi` |

The proposed short demo has one continuous story: record an account's settings flow; repeat the saved Test; introduce a visibly labeled controlled defect; show the exact unchanged check fail; repair the app; rerun the same Test; open the retained evidence. Show real waits and elapsed time. Do not present the controlled defect as a discovered customer bug, or a sped-up repeat as a latency measurement.

Suggested message to test: **“Your coding agent wrote the fix. Relay shows whether it worked.”** The supporting promise is the record → repeat → repair → review workflow, with a manual path when the agent is unavailable. A public trial needs a qualified installation entry before this becomes publishable launch copy.

Distribution should follow the demonstrated workflow: a concise clip, one working installation entry, an inspectable sample result, and a short integration guide where the target developers already work. Test completion and retained use after a week are more informative adoption measures than views. No outreach, publication or customer-data sharing was performed in this pass.

## Unknowns and decision limits

There is no verified conversion funnel, revenue, customer count, churn, paid promotion attribution or comparative reliability benchmark here. Vendor case studies show buying concerns, not representative outcomes. Public views are distribution evidence. Million-person love and paid migration cannot be guaranteed by copying features or polishing a demo. The next honest milestone is a real newcomer succeeding and a real buyer repeating one useful task with less work.
