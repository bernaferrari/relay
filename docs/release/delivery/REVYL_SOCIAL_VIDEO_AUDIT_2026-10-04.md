# Revyl public social and video audit — October 4, 2026

## Scope and evidence

The official handle is **[@tryrevyl](https://x.com/tryrevyl)**, verified through the X link in the [Revyl website](https://revyl.com/) footer. On the observed profile, X showed 1,966 followers, 388 posts and 51 photos/videos. These are a dated public snapshot, not account analytics.

The review used independent in-app browser tabs and the supported X player UI. The Videos feed reached its visible bottom at August 14, 2025. The Posts feed reached its visible bottom at June 5, 2025, with a repeated bottom scroll producing no additional company posts. Company-authored native posts, quoted videos and known launch-thread replies were inventoried. Ads, recommendations and unrelated authors' replies were excluded. This is a bounded visible corpus; deleted, unavailable, unlisted and other inaccessible material cannot be certified complete.

**143 known video-bearing company post URLs were attempted. 142 produced decoded visual samples: 132 with opening/middle/near-end coverage and 10 with partial coverage. One remained poster-only.** Multiple URLs reuse visibly matching footage; 143 is a post count, not a count of unique video assets. Two additional GIFs embedded in a customer article were partially visually sampled. The website's embedded player reported that media could not play.

This is a **visual audit, not full spoken-content watching**. No audio was transcribed or evaluated. Samples generally seek to the opening, approximately halfway, and approximately 90% through the clip. Actual observed timestamps appear below. A `readyState: 4` player plus the rendered frame was required to treat a seek as decoded; a seek timestamp with a held buffering frame was not accepted as new visual evidence. Longer clips and interviews therefore have limited coverage. All displayed metrics below are **company post views**, not unique viewers, completed video views, retention or organic reach. Dates follow X's local display; dates are 2026 unless explicitly marked 2025. Counts were observed October 4 and can change.

## What matters for the million-view question

Revyl **has visible million-view posts**. Its June 4 [Parrot customer article post](https://x.com/tryrevyl/status/2062700910950969749) showed **1,487,139 views**, 10 likes, 2 reposts, 7 bookmarks and 1 reply. Its [company quote of the same story](https://x.com/tryrevyl/status/2062702806117835259) showed **1,250,317 views**, 77 likes, 6 reposts, 16 bookmarks and 9 replies. These are public post views, not proof of million completed video watches. The [article](https://x.com/tryrevyl/article/2062700910950969749) was read in full; its embedded GIF coverage is recorded separately below.

The story connects the product to a named customer's workflow: agents write mobile code, then use a live cloud device and runtime evidence to validate and iterate. It attributes a quote to Parrot cofounder Erik Dahl and describes app-state setup, authentication, performance/network/state feedback and goal-directed iteration. This is a vendor-authored customer story; the stated results were not independently reproduced.

By comparison, the largest native product clip in the observed Videos feed was the [August 24 cloud iOS development post](https://x.com/tryrevyl/status/2092018292634620298), at **32,223 views**, 338 likes, 45 reposts and 232 bookmarks. Many feature clips and company quotes were in the hundreds or low thousands. The [Markup launch promo](https://x.com/tryrevyl/status/2092298703533134192) had **7,297 views**, while the [reply containing the concrete agent/PR outcome](https://x.com/tryrevyl/status/2092298735384682775) had **271**. This does not measure audience retention, but it does show that reply-only proof had far fewer public post views in this example.

**Inference:** copying every feature is insufficient to explain or produce million-view distribution. Revyl demonstrates product depth repeatedly with modest reach. Its broad platform hook (iOS development from Windows/Linux) and its named customer story are stronger content candidates than weekly feature lists. Public data cannot establish the effect of paid distribution, founder networks, timing, retention, account history or X's ranking. No causal diagnosis or one-million-view guarantee follows from this audit.

Calling those missing solely because a competitor demonstrates them would be incorrect.

## Content changes suggested by the evidence

1. Build clips around one understandable outcome: a broken cart corrected, a feature rendered on a phone, an animation checked, or a recorded session converted and replayed. Give the outcome prominence early and show the final proof.
2. Use familiar pain and audience hooks that the footage can support: waiting for mobile builds, verifying agent-written code, reviewing a PR, or tracing a flaky checkout. The cloud-from-Windows clip is an example of a broad, concrete constraint being removed.
3. Make runtime proof readable at normal feed size. Several long clips begin with a talking head or dense terminal. A close crop of the relevant phone, step and result carries more information per second.
4. Promote named customer outcomes when available and authorized. The million-view Parrot story is a useful candidate format, but one story with high public post views is not evidence that the format caused the reach.
5. Keep product gaps separate from publishing gaps. Runtime instrumentation, interactive previews and annotation-to-PR proof warrant product review; historical DORA dashboards or every specialist test feature should not become a viral-content checklist.

Reach analysis requires the user's own publishing history, campaign data and retention metrics to move beyond these public comparisons.
